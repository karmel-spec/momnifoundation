The Mission

The world is short on care. Mothers aren't.

Right now, more than 350 million children below primary-school age don't have access to the childcare they need. That's not a statistic to us — that's millions of mothers lying awake doing impossible math: work or watch the baby, eat or stay home.

The Momni Foundation exists because we believe the answer to a mother's problem has always been another mother. Every hour of care shared in the Momni community helps fund an hour of care for a mama in one of the world's hardest places — a market vendor in Nairobi, a seamstress in Dhaka, a mama anywhere who just needs someone to hold the baby so she can hold her life together.

No middle layers. No overhead empire. Just the Circle, reaching around the world — mother to mother, hour for hour.

The Founding Image

What the elephants taught us

When elephants sense that one of their own is vulnerable — a new mother, a wobbly calf, an injured friend — they don't form a committee. The mothers simply move. They circle up, shoulder to shoulder, facing outward, with every calf tucked safely at the center.

Nobody assigns positions. Nobody asks whose calf it is. The circle just forms, because that's what mothers do.

Karmel saw that image years ago and never let it go. It became Momni: mothers circling up around each other's children as if they were their own. And it became this Foundation — because a circle that stops at a border was never really a circle at all.

When one of us is vulnerable, all of us circle up.
